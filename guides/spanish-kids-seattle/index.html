Practicing Spanish with kids in Seattle

By Christina Green · November 20, 2026

Two things keep me in product leadership. I get to help people do things they didn’t think they could do, and I always have more to learn myself. In Range, David Epstein makes the case that experience in one field can help you solve unfamiliar problems in another. For me, Spanish is a good example.

I’m working on becoming more fluent. I know what it feels like to have a good question and only half the words to ask it. That experience shapes how I listen when someone struggles with a product my team knows inside out. I’m quicker to ask what they were trying to do and give them room to explain it in their own words.

I want my kids to see their bilingualism as a superpower they can share. Other language learners and I come back to the same question. How do we give kids more chances to use their Spanish when so much of life around them happens in English? I shared these ideas with a few peers in Seattle. One asked for a link to pass along, so I put them here.

I looked for things families can do outside school hours, with an emphasis on free or low-cost options where you can feel comfortable joining in at any level of Spanish. In each one, your kid gets to make a choice. Hand them the menu and let them order your coffee. Or let them pick a comic, claim their favorite character and tell you which part you get to read.

Play a game you already know

Pull out Spot It! and name the matching picture in Spanish. If neither of you knows the word, look it up together and see who spots it next time. For Hedbanz, two questions can take you a long way: ¿Soy un animal? ¿Se me puede comer? (Am I an animal? Can you eat me?) More game ideas from Spanish Playground.

Let your kid pick the book

Graphic novels give you something to point to when a word is unfamiliar. Let your kid choose the comic and claim a character, then read the speech bubbles to each other. Look for kids’ graphic novels in Spanish at Seattle Public Library.

Give your kid a reason to do the talking

At Olé Café, practice the order on the way in, then let your kid order for you. Para mí, un café, por favor. ¿Y para ti? works even if the rest of the conversation switches back to English.

For a grocery outing, write a short shopping list in Spanish and let your kid find the ingredients at Mendoza’s Mexican Mercado. Or let them plan the outing themselves. Browse the makers at Aquí Mercado or pick a place from Intentionalist’s Latino-owned business directory.

Take Spanish to the water

The Seattle Aquarium has offered animal talks in Spanish during ¡Vamos al Acuario! Check the Aquarium calendar for upcoming programs. For a beach day of your own, save the Aquarium’s English–Spanish field guide and let your kid be the one to identify what you find.

Get help with a homework question

When you’re both stuck on a school assignment, Seattle students in grades K–12 can get free online tutoring in Spanish with a library card or Library Link number. Find a tutor through Seattle Public Library.

Connect with your community

If your family has a job around the house that you’re ready to pay someone to do, you can hire a worker through Casa Latina. Kids can help welcome someone with a buenos días while adults handle the job details. If you’d like to give your time, start with the volunteer roles Casa Latina is asking people to fill.
